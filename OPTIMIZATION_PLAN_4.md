Status: executed

## 1. Goal

Same Pareto rule as the last three cycles: an optimisation counts only when
it moves a measured speed/precision frontier without weakening a contract.
This cycle's specific outcomes, in priority order:

1. **Close the last genuine exact-group kernel gap: `round`.** It is the
   only cheap row below parity *at 4096*, where cycle 3's memory-bandwidth
   Documented Accept does not apply: dispersion median **0.84x**
   (band 0.80–0.88), so parity sits well outside the row's own noise. All
   five corpora agree, and the `fast` metric (0.71x at 1 M in-domain) is
   just as bad. This is a kernel-body problem, not a traversal one (§5.1).
2. **Attack the repair-path density cliff structurally.** `repair.csv`
   shows `exp` going 0.82 → 2.99 ns/elem as density rises 0 → 100% — a
   3.6x cliff — while `erf`'s curve is flat. That one mechanism accounts
   for the largest remaining class of below-parity numbers: ~10 exact rows
   below 0.71x on the boundary corpus (`cosh` 0.30x, `acosh` 0.33x,
   `sinh` 0.54x, `expm1` 0.55x, …) and the random-bit disasters
   (`erfc` exact 0.44x, `y0`/`y1` fast **0.21–0.22x**). Cycle 3 closed
   individual rows with corpus-annotated accepts; cycle 4 goes after the
   mechanism (§5.2).
3. **Retire the `pow` extreme-exponent item permanently.** 8 asserted /
   5 measured has now been carried through *three* cycles. Cycle-3 P2
   added the second-order correction and verified at 10 M samples, but
   neither tightened the bound nor wrote the priced-and-declined close
   into `pow.rs` that its own acceptance criteria demanded. This cycle
   ends with the item off the books, one way or the other (§6.1).
4. **Pay down precision-verification debt.** Cycle-3 P3's sweep ran at
   10 M samples where the standing protocol floor is 30 M, and exhaustive
   f32 coverage exists for exactly two kernels (`log2f`, `erff`). Every
   asserted bound gets re-verified at protocol strength; tightening only
   where the upgraded evidence shows ≥ 2x headroom (§6.2).
5. **Make the 1 M cheap-row measurements falsifiable.** Recorded
   dispersion at 1 M is up to **130%** (`round`), 49% (`floor`) — bands so
   wide that no accept/reject on those rows can mean anything. Fix the
   measurement or stop quoting the numbers (§7).

## 2. Contracts that must not change

Carried over verbatim from the last three cycles; they remain hard:

- `BitExact` matches the platform bit-for-bit, including signed zero,
  infinities, NaN payloads, and FMA placement read from disassembly — not
  source-level equivalence.
- Every `Fast` bound is asserted in `tests/accuracy.rs`, quoted identically
  in the kernel doc and README, and may only be tightened after a measured
  campaign (30 M+ samples for `f64`, exhaustive for `f32`).
- `FullRange` is correct for all inputs; `Finite` skips checks only inside
  its documented domain.
- Tables and coefficients are generated by `tools/` from pinned upstream
  commits; regeneration is byte-identical.
- Rare lanes are repaired by `reference/` through `patch_lanes`; the vector
  main path never owns the hard cases. (§5.2 may change *how* the repair
  loop is driven, but never *who* computes the repaired value.)
- A kernel whose acceptance test is `lb == ub` keeps its full-corpus
  bit-exact test so a platform drift fails loudly.
- Acceptance evidence is end-to-end (`examples/bench.rs` rows via
  `tools/bench_matrix.py`). The `traversal.csv` ladder, `repair.csv`
  density curve, and `dispersion.json` bands are *diagnostic* evidence
  only — they attribute cost and size the noise; only function rows accept
  a change. This bites hardest in §5.2, whose natural instrument is the
  repair ladder: the ladder may *guide* the work, but acceptance is the
  boundary- and random-bit-corpus function rows.

## 5. Speed workstreams

### 5.1 S1: the `round` kernel body — P1, small to diagnose, medium to fix

The one cheap row that is *not* explained by memory bandwidth: 0.84x
median at 4096 (compute-bound regime, band 0.80–0.88), 0.52–0.71x at 1 M
across all corpora, `fast` no better than exact. The body
(`src/kernels/exact.rs::round::eval`) builds C's ties-away semantics from
`trunc` + exact fraction + compare + `copysign` bump + add — six vector
ops plus a `select`, against a scalar baseline whose `f64::round` LLVM
auto-vectorises for the plain loop.

Diagnosis first:

1. `objdump` both loops in the bench binary: count ops and check what the
   baseline's auto-vectorised `llvm.round` lowers to on Zen 5 (AVX-512
   `vrndscalepd` has no ties-away mode, so the baseline must also be
   synthesising — find out with what, and how many ops).
2. Check the `select`/mask lowering: if `bump` compiles to a mask register
   round-trip rather than a blend or bitwise trick, that is recoverable
   without touching semantics.

Fix candidates, strictly after the diagnosis names the cost:

- Rewrite the bump arithmetically without a `select`: the comparison mask
  as all-ones bits, ANDed with `1.0 | signbit(x)` — three bitwise ops,
  no blend.
- Port glibc's branchless bit-twiddled `round` lane-parallel (exponent
  arithmetic on the representation), if it is shorter than the
  trunc-and-bump form.
- Do **not** reach for the `x + copysign(0.5 − ulp, x)` single-add trick:
  it double-rounds (`0.49999999999999994` and `0.5` both break it) and
  cannot be bit-exact.

Acceptance: `round` exact and fast ≥ 0.95x at 4096 on all five corpora
(the compute-bound regime, where the row's band is 8–13%); the 1 M rows
judged only against their recorded dispersion bands. Bit-exactness suite
extended with the ties boundary neighbourhood (`±0.5 ± 2` ulp, `±2^52 ± 2`
ulp, signed zeros, subnormals, NaN payload) at widths 1/2/4/8, plus an
exhaustive f32 `roundf` pass. If the diagnosis shows the baseline's own
sequence is genuinely shorter than any bit-exact vector synthesis, close
with a Documented Accept quoting the op counts — but given the fast
metric is also below parity, that outcome is not expected.

### 5.2 S2: the repair-density cliff — P1, large, the structural item

The mechanism: `dispatch`/`patch_lanes` (`src/simd/mod.rs:419`) runs the
vector main path unconditionally, then — per vector — branches on the
rejection mask and repairs set lanes in a scalar bit-loop. Three costs
compound as density rises: the per-vector branch mispredicts, the scalar
loop serialises, and at high density the already-computed vector result is
thrown away (`mask.all()` → `map_lanes`, having paid for the main path
first). That last effect is how `y0`/`y1` fast reach **0.21x** on
random-bit: near-total rejection means the "vector" path costs the scalar
baseline *plus* a wasted vector evaluation *plus* per-vector branching.

Work in three independently-gated steps, never combined in one comparison:

1. **Instrument density.** Extend the bench's repair suite to report the
   measured mask-fire rate per (function, corpus) — one diagnostic CSV
   column. This turns every "suspect: repair density" cell in §3 into a
   number and identifies which rows step 2 and 3 can help.
2. **Batch the repair loop at slice level.** Prototype in
   `Function::eval_slice` (`src/function.rs`): the vector loop stores
   main-path results and accumulates rejected lane *indices* into a small
   buffer (bitmask per chunk suffices); a second pass repairs the
   collected indices in one tight scalar loop. The hot loop loses its
   branch entirely; repair cost becomes proportional to density with no
   per-vector penalty. The repaired *value* still comes from
   `reference::*` — the contract's repair ownership is untouched, and
   bits cannot change anywhere.
   - Gate (diagnostic): the `repair.csv` ladder's 12–50% points for `exp`
     improve materially with the 0% point within noise.
   - Gate (acceptance): boundary-corpus exact rows for
     `cosh`/`sinh`/`expm1`/`tanh`/`atanh` improve ≥ 10% median-of-
     alternating-runs, in-domain rows within 3% or band, bit-exact suites
     green at all widths.
3. **High-density bail-out.** For the `y0`/`y1`-shape disaster
   (density ≈ 100%), evaluate a cheap *pre*-check: when the rejection
   mask of the previous chunk was full, test the mask *before* running
   the main path for the next chunk. Costs one mask evaluation on the
   happy path; saves the whole wasted vector evaluation on hostile
   buffers. Only worth it if step 1 shows sustained near-1.0 density is
   what random-bit produces (likely: random 64-bit patterns are mostly
   NaN/huge). Gate: random-bit `y0`/`y1`/`erfc` fast rows recover to
   ≥ 0.9x with in-domain rows within 3%; if the happy-path cost exceeds
   that, drop it and close those rows with a corpus-annotated Documented
   Accept ("random bit patterns are outside every fast domain; the
   scalar baseline is optimal there").

`erf`/`erfc` random-bit exact (0.64x/0.44x) ride on the same steps; if
they remain below parity after 2–3, annotate them in the README's
correctly-rounded paragraph (the accurate-path rate on garbage bits is a
property of the CORE-MATH design, same story as cycle 2's S4).

### 5.3 S3: `acosh` exact on the coherent corpus — P2, small, finish S5

Cycle-3 S5's bypass lifted `/F` coherent to 1.22x but exact sits at 0.84x
and no accept is on record. The delta between the two policies is the
`FullRange` domain check + repair wiring on a corpus with zero rejections
— exactly what S2's step 2 removes from the hot loop. Order this after
S2 and re-measure; if the row is still below parity, either extend the
band-uniform bypass to cover the checked path's mask evaluation, or write
the Documented Accept cycle-3 S5 owed ("band-coherent buffers under
`FullRange` are the kernel's worst case: number, corpus, size"), quoting
both policies in the corpus-duality table.

Acceptance: coherent exact ≥ 1.0x at ≤ 3% in-domain cost, or the accept
on record in `hyper.rs`'s module doc and the README table.

### 5.4 S4: the `sqrtf` in-domain inversion — P3, small, diagnose only

`sqrtf` exact: 0.80x on in-domain, 1.07–1.15x on all four other corpora —
the only row whose *friendly* corpus is its worst. `sqrtf` delegates
(single-instruction body), so the suspects are: the in-domain corpus

## 7. Measurement protocol

Unchanged from cycle 3 (§7 there), with three amendments:

- **The 1 M cheap-row regime is observation-only.** Recorded dispersion
  (49–130% on `floor`/`round`) makes sub-ns rows at 1 M unfalsifiable as
  currently measured. Headline gates for any kernel whose 4096 ns/elem is
  below ~0.5 move to 4096 (compute-bound, bands 1–13%); 1 M numbers are
  reported with their band and the memory-bound annotation, never gated
  on. If a 1 M claim is ever *needed*, the measurement must first be
  fixed (pinned frequency, isolated cores, longer windows) and a fresh
  band recorded to show it worked.
- **Repair changes gate on two corpora.** Anything under §5.2 is accepted
  on boundary *and* random-bit function rows together, with in-domain
  within 3% or band — the ladder and density instrumentation remain
  diagnostic per the §2 contract.
- **Every targeted row has a pre-recorded band.** Phase 0.4 exists so
  that no §5/§6 claim compares against a single-run number. A claimed win
  inside the row's band is not a win — carried from cycle 3, now with the
  bands to enforce it.

Acceptance thresholds carry over: ≥ 10% on the targeted row (≥ 5% for a
shared primitive), no unrelated row worse than 3% (or its dispersion band
if wider), correctness suites green at every width, f32 changes
exhaustive.
